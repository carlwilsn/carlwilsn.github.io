The [first post today]({% post_url 2026-05-30-day1-bitnet-plateau %}) was the science — a ternary model that flatlined, and the controlled A/B that turned a dead run into a real result. This post is the other half of day one, the part that doesn't show up in a loss curve: **the setup.** Because here's the honest thing — I didn't type most of it. I directed an agent that did.

## What "I had it build everything" actually means

Day one had a lot of moving parts, and an agent built essentially all of them under my direction:

- The public training repo — [`bitnet-repro`](https://github.com/carlwilsn/bitnet-repro) — harness, `BitLinear`, the experiment scaffolding.
- The cloud GPU plumbing: a Lambda A10, SSH keys registered, the agent connection working, the box reachable on demand.
- This blog — the three-column layout, the Open Questions and Revelations rails, the build that copies content into Jekyll so I never touch the theme internals.
- And the first post itself. The write-up of the BitNet result? Also the agent. I read the numbers and steered the framing; it wrote the prose.

So the meta-fact of day one is that the thing reproducing the paper, the thing it runs on, and the thing you're reading right now were all built by the same collaborator. I was the one with the goal and the judgment. It was the one with the hands.

## Giving it hands on the GPU

The piece I care most about: it isn't just writing code I then run. **Today it got tools** — it can drive the Lambda box directly. It resolves the instance's current IP, SSHes in, runs the command, and hands me back the output. The loop from "let's try a run" to "here are the numbers" no longer routes through my keyboard at all. I gave it hands on the GPU, and that's what made fifteen-second experiment cycles feel free.

That's a different kind of leverage than autocomplete. The bottleneck stops being *how fast can I type the plumbing* and becomes *do I know what's worth trying.*

## The paper, and what's mine to keep

I read the [BitNet b1.58 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17764) for the first time today, end to end. It's a genuinely cool idea — ternary weights, native quantization, the claim that it only pays off at scale. Reading it is mine. The taste for which experiment splits "my setup is broken" from "the method is limited" is mine. The decision to spend day one proving out the *whole loop* before spending real money is mine.

What's changing is the ratio. The grunt work — boilerplate, deploy configs, SSH dances, even first drafts — compresses toward zero, and what's left is the part that was always the point: knowing what to build and why. Day one was me learning to work that way. The setup is the real artifact.
